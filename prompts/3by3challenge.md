## Objective

This challenge is designed to assess participants’ fundamental understanding of Rhino and Grasshopper, as well as their computational thinking in developing original parametric forms and geometries.

Participants are expected to demonstrate:

- Core Rhino 3D modeling skills
- Fundamental Grasshopper workflow and logic
- Clear parametric thinking and control
- Creative exploration of form and geometry
- Ability to produce an original design outcome without relying on known generative algorithms

### Challenge Description

Design a series of 9 unique parametric objects arranged in a 3x3 grid.

Each object should express a distinct geometric idea while showing a coherent design logic across the full set. The outcome should demonstrate both technical control and creativity in the use of Rhino and Grasshopper.

###   Requirements

- Create 9 objects in total, arranged in a 3x3 layout
- Each object must fit within a maximum bounding box of 3000 mm x 3000 mm x 3000 mm
- Each object must be positioned at the center of a 5 m x 5 m grid cell
- The full 3x3 arrangement will therefore occupy a total footprint of 15 m x 15 m
- All geometry must be modeled in Rhino and developed through Grasshopper
- The design must be parametric and adjustable through clearly defined inputs

### Design Rules

- Do not use any known generative algorithm or pre-established procedural system
- Avoid standard examples such as Voronoi, L-systems, fractals, reaction-diffusion, attractor clichés, recursive subdivisions, or other recognizable algorithm-driven form-making methods
- Focus instead on original rule sets, geometric relationships, spatial logic, or custom parametric operations
- The emphasis is on creative thinking, not algorithmic imitation

### Evaluation Criteria

Entries will be assessed based on the following:

1. Fundamental Rhino Modeling Skills
- Accuracy and cleanliness of geometry
- Appropriate use of surfaces, solids, curves, and construction logic

2. Grasshopper Understanding
- Clear definition structure
- Logical data flow
- Effective use of parameters, controls, and relationships
- Readability and organization of the Grasshopper file

3. Computational Thinking
- Evidence of structured rule-based design thinking
- Ability to translate geometric ideas into parametric systems
- Intelligent control over variation and dependency

4. Creativity and Originality
- Inventiveness of geometric concept
- Departure from familiar or overused generative methods
- Strength of formal exploration

5. Spatial Composition
- Quality of the overall 3x3 arrangement
- Relationship between individual objects and the full field
- Balance between variation and coherence

### Criteria for each Object
1. Compression and Release
"Design a parametric object that explores the idea of compression and release through geometry. The form should appear dense, constrained, or compact in some areas and open, stretched, or expanded in others. Use controllable geometric rules rather than known generative algorithms. The object must fit within a 3000 mm x 3000 mm x 3000 mm bounding box and be suitable for placement at the center of a 5 m x 5 m grid cell."

2. Twisted Frame Logic
"Generate a parametric object based on the transformation of a simple frame or scaffold. The object should evolve through rotation, offset, misalignment, thickening, or bending of structural elements. Avoid recognizable algorithmic patterns and instead create an original geometric system with clear parametric controls. The final object must fit within 3000 mm in all dimensions."

3. Surface Tension Without Simulation
"Create a form that suggests stretching, pulling, or surface tension, but do not use physics simulations or known generative systems. Build the geometry through explicit parametric relationships in Grasshopper. The object should feel dynamic and spatially rich while remaining controlled and constructible within a 3000 mm cube."

4. Carved Mass
"Design a parametric object that begins as a solid mass and is transformed through a sequence of subtractive geometric operations. Focus on rhythm, void, depth, and internal spatial relationships. Avoid standard paneling or recursive operations. The result should be an original carved form that fits inside a 3000 mm x 3000 mm x 3000 mm boundary."

5. Directional Growth Without Organic Algorithms
"Generate a parametric geometry that appears to grow in a specific direction or along multiple directional forces, but do not use botanical, fractal, branching, or agent-based systems. Use custom geometric logic to create a sense of movement, expansion, or drift. The object must remain bounded within 3000 mm in each axis."

6. Interlocking Geometric Families
"Create a parametric object composed of multiple related parts that interlock, overlap, nest, or weave together. Each part should derive from a shared rule set while producing a complex overall figure. Avoid tessellation clichés and standard generative patterns. Keep the entire object within a 3000 mm cube."

7. Controlled Irregularity
"Design a parametric object that balances order and irregularity. The form should contain enough logic to feel intentional, but enough variation to feel unexpected. Use explicit, controllable parameters to produce non-repetitive geometry without relying on randomization as the primary driver. The object must fit within 3000 mm x 3000 mm x 3000 mm."

8. Geometric Echoes
"Generate a form in which one geometric action is repeated with progressive transformation, producing echoes, traces, or afterimages in space. The repetition should not read as a standard array or recursive system, but as a carefully controlled family of related conditions. The object should be original, spatially layered, and bounded within 3000 mm."

9. Nine-Part Family Logic
"Develop a parametric system for a family of 9 related objects arranged in a 3x3 grid. Each object should share a common geometric DNA but express a distinct variation in proportion, density, orientation, carving, or structural behavior. Each object must fit within a 3000 mm cube and sit at the center of a 5 m x 5 m grid cell. Avoid all known generative algorithms and focus on an original rule-based design language."
